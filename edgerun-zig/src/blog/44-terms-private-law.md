# You Do Not Negotiate With Apps. You Accept Private Law.

The modern internet is full of contracts people cannot read, cannot negotiate, and cannot realistically refuse.

Terms of service define what users can do, what can be deleted, how data can be used, what disputes mean, whether arbitration applies, and when access can vanish.

> Mental model: private law gets weaker when users can actually leave with identity and history intact.

## Private law

Terms can control:

- data use
- account termination
- content rules
- business models
- dispute process
- arbitration
- API access
- app behavior
- export rights

That makes terms a private rulebook for ordinary life. The user does not negotiate it. The user accepts it because friends, work, school, banks, landlords, customers, or governments already depend on the service.

## Why consent gets weak

Consent is meaningful when refusal is realistic. It gets weaker when:

- the service is socially required
- the terms change unilaterally
- data cannot be exported
- identity cannot move
- purchases cannot move
- disputes go to forced arbitration
- deletion does not delete derived data

The button says "I agree." The reality is often "I need access."

## Better contract shape

Technical design can reduce private-law power:

```text
portable identity -> replaceable app
portable data -> credible exit
signed receipts -> evidence
open protocol -> alternative clients
local storage -> less hostage value
```

When users can leave without losing their history, terms become less absolute.
