# What Owning a Phone Should Mean

A real personal computer should obey the person first.

Owning a device should mean more than holding the glass rectangle. It should mean authority over installation, data, repair, identity, compute, and consent.

## Ownership should include

- installing software from sources you choose
- inspecting what has access to your data
- exporting your data fully
- running local apps without cloud permission
- replacing the OS when vendor support ends
- repairing hardware
- using your compute for yourself or renting and sharing it
- proving who you are without renting identity from a platform
- encrypting data to people and devices, not companies
- revoking permissions and seeing what happened

## Interactive demo

An ownership rights checklist compares today's common phone model with a user-owned runtime. Each right lights up the authority required: device key, app capability, local storage, repair path, package signature, sealed object route, or audit log.

## Main lesson

The device should serve the person before the platform, carrier, store, vendor, or remote service.

## EdgeRun seed

EdgeRun should make ownership concrete: user keys, local-first storage, signed apps, explicit capabilities, inspectable execution, sealed sharing, and logs that explain what happened.
