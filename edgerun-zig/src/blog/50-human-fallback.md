# Automation Is Great Until There Is No Human Left To Appeal To

A system with no appeal path is not efficient. It is brittle.

Automated systems remove humans for speed and cost. That works until an account is banned, a bank freezes funds, delivery support loops forever, identity verification fails, a SIM is lost, or a platform locks someone out.

> Mental model: automation is brittle when there is no evidence, appeal, or human fallback.

## Failure without appeal

- account ban
- bank freeze
- SIM loss
- delivery dispute
- identity check failure
- payment block
- app store rejection
- fraud score mistake

The common pattern is not that automation made a mistake. Humans make mistakes too. The pattern is that the system removed every path for correction.

## Why fallback matters

Critical services are messy because people are messy:

- names change
- phones are stolen
- families share devices
- documents expire
- addresses move
- networks fail
- fraud systems overreact
- accounts get confused

An automated system can handle the common path. It should not be the only path.

## What a real fallback looks like

A real fallback has more than a chat bot:

- exportable data
- signed receipts
- human review
- physical office or postal path for critical services
- portable identity proof
- appeal deadlines
- reason codes
- evidence the user can keep

Without evidence, the user cannot appeal. Without appeal, the service is not accountable.
